# NYC Supportive Housing Resources

## What is Supportive Housing?
Supportive housing combines affordable housing with on-site services for people who need support to live independently — including those experiencing homelessness, living with HIV/AIDS, serious mental illness, substance use disorders, or other special needs.

## Types of Supportive Housing in NYC

### 1. NYC Supportive Housing Commission (SHC) Units
- **What:** Permanent supportive housing units funded through the NYC 15/15 plan
- **Who:** Chronically homeless individuals, families, young adults
- **Services:** Case management, mental health, employment, health services
- **How to apply:** Through Coordinated Assessment and Placement System (CAPS)
- **Contact:** Call 311 or visit a DROP-IN center

### 2. HASA Housing (HIV/AIDS Services Administration)
- **What:** Housing for NYC residents diagnosed with HIV or AIDS
- **Who:** Must have HIV/AIDS diagnosis; NYC resident
- **Services:** Case management, rental assistance, home care, vocational services
- **How to apply:** Contact HASA ServiceLine
  - **Phone:** 718-557-1399
  - **Email:** ServiceLine@hra.nyc.gov
  - **Location:** 400 Eighth Avenue, 2nd Floor, NY 10001
  - **Hours:** Mon-Fri 9 AM - 5 PM
- **Scattered sites:** HASA contracts with nonprofits for scattered-site apartments across Manhattan and other boroughs

### 3. NYCHA Supportive Housing
- **What:** NYCHA sets aside units for referrals from city agencies
- **Who:** Homeless families (DHS referrals), HASA clients, veterans (VASH), ACS referrals
- **Priority codes:** N0 (city agency referrals), N1 (domestic violence), W0 (homeless working families)
- **How to apply:** Must be referred by DHS, HASA, ACS, or other city agency

### 4. HUD-VASH (Veterans)
- **What:** Section 8 vouchers + VA supportive services for homeless veterans
- **Who:** Eligible low-income veterans experiencing homelessness
- **Contact:** VA NY Harbor Healthcare System — (877) 4AID-VET
- **Services:** Case management, healthcare, mental health services

### 5. SPOA / SPOC (Single Point of Access)
- **What:** Pathway to supportive housing for adults with serious mental illness (SMI)
- **Who:** Adults 18+ with SMI who are homeless or at risk
- **How to apply:** Through NYC DOHMH SPOA
- **Contact:** 311 — ask for "SPOA" or "Single Point of Access"
- **Scattered sites:** Many SPOA placements are in scattered-site apartments

## Manhattan Scattered-Site Supportive Housing

Scattered-site housing places individuals in apartments throughout regular residential buildings, with support services provided by a nonprofit agency. This is different from congregate supportive housing (single building with all supportive units).

### How Scattered Sites Work
1. Nonprofit leases apartments from private landlords across Manhattan
2. Tenant pays 30% of income toward rent (if any); program covers the rest
3. Nonprofit provides case management, service coordination, and support
4. Units are integrated into regular buildings — no institutional setting

### Key Providers with Manhattan Scattered Sites
- **CUCS** — Largest provider of NYC supportive housing, operates scattered-site units across Manhattan
- **Pathways to Housing** — Housing First model, scattered-site apartments for chronically homeless
- **Community Access** — Mental health focused, scattered-site apartments in Manhattan
- **Bailey House** — HIV/AIDS scattered-site housing in Manhattan
- **Breaking Ground** — Scattered-site units for formerly homeless individuals

### How to Access Scattered-Site Housing
1. **For homeless individuals:** Contact a street outreach team or visit a DROP-IN center
2. **For mental health:** Apply through SPOA (call 311)
3. **For HIV/AIDS:** Apply through HASA (718-557-1399)
4. **For veterans:** Contact VA VASH program
5. **For families:** Contact DHS through 311 or a Homebase office

## CAPS (Coordinated Assessment and Placement System)
NYC's centralized system for matching people to supportive housing:
- All supportive housing referrals go through CAPS
- Applicants are assessed and matched to appropriate units
- Contact: 311 — ask for "supportive housing assessment"

## Emergency Resources

- **Homebase** — Homelessness prevention, eviction assistance
  - Find your local office at nyc.gov/homebase
- **DHS Shelter Intake** — For immediate shelter needs
  - Single adults: 400-430 East 30th Street, Manhattan
  - Families with children: 151 East 151st Street, Bronx
- **NYC Well** — Free confidential mental health support: 1-888-692-9355
