## 1. Account Setup

### Creating Your NYC Housing Connect Account

Go to **[housingconnect.nyc.gov](https://housingconnect.nyc.gov)** and click "Create an Account." You'll need an email address that you check regularly — all lottery communications come through email.

### Information You'll Need to Provide

| Category | Details |
|----------|---------|
| **Personal Info** | Full legal name, date of birth, Social Security Number (SSN) or Individual Taxpayer Identification Number (ITIN) |
| **Household Composition** | Every person who will live with you — their full names, dates of birth, SSNs/ITINs, and relationship to you |
| **Income** | Total gross (pre-tax) household income from ALL sources for the past 12 months, including wages, self-employment, benefits, pensions, child support, etc. |
| **Current Address** | Where you currently live — street address, apartment number, city, state, ZIP |
| **Current Rent** | How much you currently pay in rent (if renting) |
| **Landlord Info** | Current landlord name and phone number |
| **Preferences** | Whether you qualify for any community preferences (see Section 4) |
| **Accessibility Needs** | Whether anyone in your household needs mobility, vision, or hearing-accessible units |

### Important Notes

- **Be 100% accurate.** Any discrepancy between your application and your documents can disqualify you.
- **Include EVERYONE** who will live in the apartment. Adding people later is not allowed.
- **Use your legal name** exactly as it appears on your ID and SSN card.
- **One account per household.** Do not create multiple accounts.
- **You can apply to multiple lotteries** with a single account.
- **No fee.** NYC Housing Connect is completely free. Anyone who charges you to apply is scamming you.

### If You Don't Have a SSN

You can use an **Individual Taxpayer Identification Number (ITIN)** instead. All household members who have a SSN must provide it; those who don't can provide an ITIN or leave it blank (for young children).

## 2. Documents Needed

When your lottery number is called, you'll need to submit documentation within a strict timeframe (usually 10-30 days). **Start gathering these now** and keep them updated.

### Income Verification

| Document | Who Needs It |
|----------|-------------|
| **Pay stubs** — Most recent 6 consecutive pay stubs | All employed household members 18+ |
| **Tax returns** — Most recent federal/state tax return + W-2s/1099s | All household members who filed taxes |
| **Self-employment proof** — Profit/loss statement, 1099s, business bank statements | Self-employed household members |
| **Benefit award letters** — SSA, SSI, SSDI, Veterans benefits, pension, unemployment | Anyone receiving benefits |
| **Child support documentation** — Court order or payment records | Anyone receiving child support |
| **Zero-Income Affidavit** — Sworn statement of no income | Household members with $0 income (see Section 7) |

### Identity Documents

| Document | Who Needs It |
|----------|-------------|
| **Photo ID** — Driver's license, state ID, passport, or NYC ID | All household members 18+ |
| **Social Security cards** — Original or photocopy | All household members with SSNs |
| **Birth certificates** | All household members (especially minors) |
| **Marriage certificate** | Married couples |
| **Divorce/annulment decree** | If applicable |

### Current Housing Verification

| Document | Who Needs It |
|----------|-------------|
| **Current lease** | If you have one |
| **Utility bills** — Most recent (electric, gas, water) | All applicants |
| **Rent receipts or cancelled rent checks** | If no lease |
| **Residency affidavit** | If you cannot provide a lease or utility bills (e.g., living with family, in shelter) |

### Household Composition

| Document | Who Needs It |
|----------|-------------|
| **Marriage certificate** | Married couples |
| **Domestic partnership registration** | Registered domestic partners |
| **Custody/guardian documents** — Court order, custody agreement | For children not living with both parents |
| **Pregnancy verification** — Doctor's letter | If claiming an unborn child as a household member |
| **Full-time student verification** — Letter from school | For household members 18+ who are students |

### For No-Income / Homeless Applicants (Additional)

| Document | Purpose |
|----------|---------|
| **Zero-Income Affidavit** | Sworn statement declaring no income |
| **DHS referral letter** | For individuals/families in the NYC shelter system |
| **HASA letter** | For individuals receiving HIV/AIDS Services Administration assistance |
| **N0/N1/N4 priority documentation** | NYCHA priority codes for homeless applicants (see Section 7) |
| **SPOA referral** | Single Point of Access for mental health supportive housing |

### Document Tips

- **Keep digital copies** — Scan or photograph every document. Store them in a secure cloud service.
- **Keep originals** — Some interviews require original documents.
- **Update regularly** — Pay stubs and bank statements should be recent (within 30-60 days).
- **Consistency matters** — Names, addresses, and income figures must match across all documents.
- **Translations** — Documents in languages other than English must be certified translated.

## 3. AMI Explained

### What Is AMI?

**Area Median Income (AMI)** is a figure set by the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD) each year. It represents the midpoint income for the New York City metropolitan area. Affordable housing eligibility and rents are based on percentages of AMI.

### 2026 NYC Area AMI

The **2026 AMI** for the NYC region is **$152,700 for a three-person household** (100% AMI).

### Income Bands

| Income Band | % of AMI | Description |
|------------|----------|-------------|
| **Extremely Low-Income (ELI)** | 0-30% | Deepest affordability; highest need |
| **Very Low-Income (VLI)** | 31-50% | Major affordable housing programs |
| **Low-Income (LI)** | 51-80% | Standard affordable housing |
| **Moderate-Income (Mod)** | 81-120% | Workforce housing |
| **Middle-Income (Mid)** | 121-165% | Middle-income housing |

### 2026 Income Limits by Household Size

| Household Size | 30% AMI (ELI) | 40% AMI | 50% AMI (VLI) | 60% AMI | 80% AMI (LI) |
|---------------|---------------|---------|---------------|---------|--------------|
| **1 person** | $35,640 | $47,520 | $59,400 | $71,280 | $95,040 |
| **2 persons** | $40,710 | $54,280 | $67,850 | $81,420 | $108,560 |
| **3 persons** | $45,810 | $61,080 | $76,350 | $91,620 | $122,160 |
| **4 persons** | $50,880 | $67,840 | $84,800 | $101,760 | $135,680 |
| **5 persons** | $54,960 | $73,280 | $91,600 | $109,920 | $146,560 |
| **6 persons** | $59,040 | $78,720 | $98,400 | $118,080 | $157,440 |
| **7 persons** | $63,120 | $84,160 | $105,200 | $126,240 | $168,320 |
| **8 persons** | $67,170 | $89,560 | $111,950 | $134,340 | $179,120 |

### 2025 Maximum Affordable Monthly Rents

| Unit Size | 30% AMI | 40% AMI | 50% AMI | 60% AMI | 80% AMI |
|-----------|---------|---------|---------|---------|---------|
| **Studio** | $850 | $1,134 | $1,417 | $1,701 | $2,268 |
| **1 Bedroom** | $911 | $1,215 | $1,518 | $1,822 | $2,430 |
| **2 Bedroom** | $1,093 | $1,458 | $1,822 | $2,187 | $2,916 |
| **3 Bedroom** | $1,263 | $1,685 | $2,106 | $2,527 | $3,370 |

> **Note:** These are maximum rents. Actual rents for specific buildings may be lower. Check each listing for exact rent amounts.

### How to Use AMI Charts

1. **Find your household size** in the left column
2. **Find your annual gross income** (before taxes) in that row
3. **The column header** tells you your AMI percentage
4. **When browsing listings**, look for units at your AMI level or above (you can qualify for units at your AMI level or lower, not higher)

**Example:** A 3-person household earning $70,000/year qualifies for:
- 50% AMI units (income limit $76,350) — YES
- 60% AMI units (income limit $91,620) — YES
- 80% AMI units (income limit $122,160) — YES
- But NOT 40% AMI units (income limit $61,080) — income too high

## 4. Community Preferences

### What Are Preferences?

When you apply for a housing lottery, you can claim **preferences** if you meet certain criteria. Applicants with preferences are placed ahead of those without. If you don't qualify for any preferences, you go into the general pool.

### Preference Categories

#### Community Board Preference (50% of units)
- **Who qualifies:** Residents of the community district where the building is located
- **How it works:** 50% of units in most lotteries are set aside for community board residents
- **How to claim:** Your current address must be within the building's community district
- **Why it matters:** This is the most impactful preference — half the units go to locals
- **Check your community district:** Look up the building's community board number on the listing, then check if your address falls within that district at nyc.gov/site/planning/community/community-portal.page

#### NYC Resident Preference
- **Who qualifies:** Current NYC residents
- **How it works:** Most lotteries give preference to NYC residents over non-residents
- **Impact:** If you live in NYC, you're already covered

#### NYCHA Preference
- **Who qualifies:** Current NYCHA public housing residents
- **How it works:** Some listings set aside units for NYCHA residents
- **Impact:** Varies by listing — typically 5% of units
- **How to claim:** Provide your NYCHA development name and apartment number

#### Senior (62+) Preference
- **Who qualifies:** Households where the head, spouse, or co-head is 62 or older
- **How it works:** Senior-designated buildings and some mixed buildings give preference to seniors
- **Impact:** Can be 100% of units in senior buildings, or a set percentage in mixed buildings
- **How to claim:** Provide proof of age (ID, birth certificate)

#### Mobility/Vision/Hearing Accessibility Units
- **Who qualifies:** Households needing accessible features
- **How it works:** A percentage of units (often 5%) are built to be accessible for mobility, vision, or hearing impairments
- **Impact:** These units are set aside specifically for applicants who need them
- **How to claim:** Indicate your accessibility needs on your application; you may need to provide medical documentation during eligibility

#### Municipal Employee Preference
- **Who qualifies:** Full-time employees of the City of New York
- **How it works:** Some listings set aside units for municipal employees
- **Impact:** Varies by listing — typically 5% of units
- **How to claim:** Provide your city agency and employee ID

#### Additional Preferences (Building-Specific)
Some buildings have additional preferences:
- **Live/work preference** — for residents of a specific neighborhood
- **Displaced tenant preference** — for tenants displaced by fire, vacancy, or demolition
- **Veteran preference** — some buildings set aside units for veterans

### How Preferences Affect Your Odds

Preferences are applied **in order**. The lottery draws are processed as follows:

1. **First:** Applicants with Community Board preference + other applicable preferences
2. **Second:** Community Board preference only
3. **Third:** Other applicable preferences (NYCHA, senior, municipal, etc.)
4. **Fourth:** NYC residents with no preferences
5. **Last:** Non-NYC residents

**Key takeaway:** If you live in the same community district as a building, your odds improve dramatically because you're competing for 50% of the units among a much smaller pool of applicants.

## 5. Application Process Step-by-Step

### Step 1: Create Your Account
Go to housingconnect.nyc.gov and create an account. Fill in all household and income information accurately. This is your master profile — you'll use it for every application.

### Step 2: Find Listings
- Browse available listings at housingconnect.nyc.gov
- New listings are posted regularly — **check weekly**
- Each listing shows: building address, unit sizes, AMI levels, income requirements, rent amounts, deadline, and preferences
- You can filter by borough, unit size, and income level

### Step 3: Check Your Eligibility
For each listing, verify:
- **Income match** — Does your household income fall within the required range for any unit type?
- **Household size** — Does the unit type fit your household? (Studio: 1-2 people, 1BR: 1-3, 2BR: 2-5, 3BR: 4-7, etc.)
- **Preferences** — Do you qualify for community board, NYCHA, senior, or other preferences?
- **Accessibility** — Do you need an accessible unit?

### Step 4: Apply
- Click "Apply" on the listing page
- Review your household information (pulled from your profile)
- Confirm which unit types you're applying for
- Claim any preferences you qualify for
- Submit before the deadline
- **You will receive a confirmation email** with your lottery log number

### Step 5: What Happens After You Apply

#### If It's a Lottery
- All applications are randomly assigned a log number
- The developer/marketing agent processes applications in log number order
- They start with applicants who have preferences, then general pool
- If your log number is called, you'll be contacted by email and/or mail

#### If It's a Log (First-Come, First-Served)
- Applications are processed in the order they were received
- Earlier applications have better odds
- Some listings use a waiting list — you'll be placed on the list in order

### Step 6: Being Called for Eligibility Review
- You'll receive an email requesting documents
- **Respond immediately** — you typically have 10-30 days
- Submit all required documents (see Section 2)
- If documents are incomplete, you may get one chance to fix them
- If you miss the deadline, your application is rejected and the next person is called

### Timeline Expectations
- **Lottery open to close:** Typically 30-90 days
- **Drawing to first contact:** Can take weeks to months
- **Eligibility review to approval:** Median 191 days (about 6 months) per HPD FY2025 data
- **Total process:** Can take 6 months to 2+ years from application to move-in
- **Waitlists:** Some waitlists last years — stay patient and keep your info updated

## 6. After You Win the Lottery

### Phase 1: Document Submission
1. **You'll receive a "Requested Materials" email** listing exactly what to submit
2. **Gather everything** — do not submit partial packages
3. **Make copies** — keep originals; submit copies unless originals are specifically required
4. **Submit by the deadline** — usually 10-30 days from the email
5. **Use certified mail or hand delivery** if physical documents are required; digital submission via the Housing Connect portal is increasingly common

### Phase 2: Eligibility Interview
1. **You'll be scheduled for an interview** — in-person or virtual, depending on the development
2. **Bring all original documents** to an in-person interview
3. **All household members 18+** typically must attend or provide notarized statements
4. **What they verify:**
   - Identity (ID + SSN match)
   - Income (pay stubs + tax returns match what you reported)
   - Household composition (everyone listed actually lives with you)
   - Current housing situation
   - Preference eligibility (community board address, NYCHA status, etc.)
   - Credit history (they will run a credit check)
   - Criminal background (for some buildings)
   - Rental history (prior landlord references)

### Phase 3: Approval and Lease Signing
1. **If approved**, you'll receive an offer letter with the unit details, rent, and move-in date
2. **You typically have 3-7 days** to accept the offer
3. **Security deposit** — Usually 1 month's rent
4. **First month's rent** — Due at lease signing
5. **Lease signing** — Sign the lease, usually a 1-year initial term
6. **Move-in** — Coordinate move-in date with the building management
7. **Recertification** — Most affordable housing requires annual income recertification

### If You're Rejected
- You will receive a written notice explaining the reason
- You have the right to **appeal** — the notice will explain how
- Common rejection reasons: income mismatch, incomplete documents, credit issues, criminal history
- **Don't give up** — apply to more lotteries. Your log number in one lottery has no impact on others

### Credit Considerations
- Affordable housing landlords run credit checks, but standards are often more lenient than market-rate
- They typically look for: no recent evictions, no outstanding rent obligations, no recent bankruptcies
- **"Ready to Rent"** (see Section 9) provides free credit counseling to help you prepare

## 7. For Zero-Income Applicants

### Overview

If you have zero income (or very low income), you can still apply for affordable housing. There are specific pathways and documentation designed for this situation.

### Zero-Income Affidavit

**What it is:** A sworn, notarized statement declaring that you have no income from any source.

**Where to get it:**
- Download from nyc.gov/site/hpd (search "zero income affidavit")
- Request from a Housing Ambassador (see Section 8)
- Request from the marketing agent when contacted for eligibility

**How to fill it out:**
1. Fill in your name, address, and the date
2. State that you have no income from employment, self-employment, benefits, pensions, child support, or any other source
3. Explain how you meet your basic needs (e.g., staying with family, receiving food from pantries, in-kind support)
4. Sign in front of a notary public
5. **Notaries are available free** at many banks, USPS offices, and NYC Housing Ambassador offices

### What Counts as Income vs. Not Income

**Counts as income:**
- Wages and salaries (gross, pre-tax)
- Self-employment income
- Social Security benefits (retirement, survivors)
- SSI (Supplemental Security Income)
- SSDI (Social Security Disability Insurance)
- Unemployment benefits
- Pension/retirement income
- Child support received
- Alimony received
- Veterans benefits
- Workers' compensation
- Rental income
- Interest/dividends from investments
- Cash assistance (TANF, Safety Net Assistance)

**Does NOT count as income:**
- Food stamps (SNAP)
- WIC
- Medicaid
- HEAP (heating assistance)
- School lunch benefits
- Food pantry/food bank assistance
- In-kind gifts from family/friends (not regular cash payments)
- Federal stimulus payments
- Tax refunds
- Earned Income Tax Credit (EITC) payments
- One-time insurance settlements
- Reimbursement for work expenses

### NYCHA Priority Codes

If you are applying through NYCHA for public housing or NYCHA-preference affordable units, priority codes determine your placement:

| Code | Meaning | Priority |
|------|---------|----------|
| **N0** | Homeless — no income | Highest |
| **N1** | Homeless — with some income | High |
| **N4** | Substandard housing (no running water, no heat, severe disrepair) | High |
| **W0** | Working family, homeless | Varies |
| **W1** | Working family, substandard housing | Varies |
| **W2** | Working family, overcrowded | Varies |
| **W3** | Working family, other priority | Varies |
| **EM** | Emergency (domestic violence, hate crime, witness protection) | Highest |

**How to get a N0/N1/N4 priority:**
1. Apply at NYCHA: selfserve.nycha.info or call 718-707-7771
2. For N0/N1 (homeless): You must have a **DHS referral letter** from your shelter case worker
3. For N4 (substandard): You must have an **HPD inspection report** documenting the violations
4. Update your status with NYCHA whenever your situation changes

### DHS Referral Letters

If you are in the NYC shelter system:
1. Ask your **shelter case manager** for a DHS referral letter
2. The letter should state: your name, shelter name, date entered shelter, and that you are homeless
3. Submit this letter with your housing application and as part of your NYCHA priority documentation
4. **Homebase** can help: nyc.gov/homebase or call 311

### HASA Letters

If you receive services from the HIV/AIDS Services Administration (HASA):
1. Contact your **HASA case worker** for a letter documenting your status
2. The letter should confirm your HASA enrollment and any benefits you receive
3. Call HASA: **718-557-1399**
4. HASA-eligible clients often get priority for certain supportive housing units

### SPOA (Single Point of Access) — Mental Health Supportive Housing

If you have a serious mental illness and need supportive housing:
1. **SPOA** is the gateway to mental health supportive housing in NYC
2. Contact: **NYC DOHMH SPOA** at 212-639-7700 or nyc.gov/site/doh/health/health-topics/spon.page
3. A SPOA referral can connect you to supportive housing with on-site services
4. You'll need: a mental health assessment, proof of income (or zero-income affidavit), and a completed SPOA application
5. SPOA housing often has deeply affordable rents for ELI/zero-income applicants

### Combining Pathways

You can and should pursue multiple pathways simultaneously:
- Apply to Housing Connect lotteries (especially ELI/VLI units)
- Apply to NYCHA public housing (get your priority code assigned)
- Get a SPOA referral if you qualify for mental health supportive housing
- Work with a Housing Ambassador to navigate all options
- Contact Homebase for homelessness prevention and housing navigation

## 8. Housing Ambassadors

### What Are Housing Ambassadors?

Housing Ambassadors are community-based organizations partnered with NYC HPD to provide **FREE** assistance with Housing Connect applications. They can help you:

- Create a Housing Connect account
- Find and apply to listings
- Gather required documents
- Understand AMI and income eligibility
- Prepare for the eligibility review
- Connect with financial counseling

**Housing Ambassadors do NOT:**
- Provide housing directly
- Guarantee you'll win a lottery
- Charge any fees (their services are completely free)

### How to Find an Ambassador

1. **Online map:** HPD Housing Ambassadors Map at experience.arcgis.com/experience/c09688daed614c8ab78ed7452437e47e

## 9. Ready to Rent Program

### What Is Ready to Rent?

**Ready to Rent** is a free program that helps New Yorkers prepare to apply for affordable housing by providing:

1. **Free one-on-one financial counseling** — Check your credit, calculate your income for housing applications, and save for moving expenses
2. **Free application assistance** — Connect with a Housing Ambassador who can help you use Housing Connect, gather documents, and prepare for eligibility

### How to Access Ready to Rent

- **Book online:** Visit [nyc.gov/site/hpd/services-and-information/ready-to-rent.page](https://www.nyc.gov/site/hpd/services-and-information/ready-to-rent.page) and click "Book an appointment"
- **Call 311** and say "Ready to Rent"
- **Financial Empowerment Portal:** Create an account at a866-dcwpwb.nyc.gov/talkmoney/book-appointment
- **Contact a Housing Ambassador directly** (see Section 8)

### What to Expect

- Sessions can be conducted **over the phone or in person**
- Available in **multiple languages** including Spanish, Chinese, Bengali, Arabic, French, Haitian Creole, Korean, Polish, Russian, and Urdu
- At least one household member must be **over age 18**
- Counselors help you:
  - Check and understand your credit score
  - Dispute credit report errors
  - Calculate your household income for housing applications
  - Create a budget and savings plan for moving expenses
  - Gather required documents
  - Navigate the Housing Connect portal

### Funding

Ready to Rent is funded by the NYC City Council and is a collaborative effort between:
- **HPD** (Department of Housing Preservation and Development)
- **DCWP** (Department of Consumer and Worker Protection)
- **Ariva** (special programming partner)

The program was relaunched in 2025 with $450,000 in City Council funding, providing expanded capacity after being scaled back during COVID-19.

## 10. Application Tracking

### Why Track Your Applications?

You may apply to dozens or hundreds of lotteries over time. Without a tracking system, it's easy to lose track of deadlines, log numbers, and statuses. A tracking spreadsheet helps you:

- Remember which lotteries you've applied to
- Track deadlines for open lotteries
- Log your confirmation/log numbers
- Monitor which documents you've submitted
- Record interview dates and outcomes
- Know when to follow up

### How to Track

We've created an **Application Tracker CSV** at: housing/applications/application-tracker.csv

This spreadsheet is pre-populated with all 113 Active and Tenant Selection buildings from our target area (Manhattan, Williamsburg, Bushwick) that have extremely-low, very-low, or low-income units, sorted by deadline (most urgent first).

### Columns in the Tracker

| Column | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| **lottery_id** | HPD lottery ID number |
| **lottery_name** | Building/development name |
| **lottery_status** | Active, Tenant Selection, Closed, etc. |
| **address** | Building street address |
| **borough** | Manhattan, Brooklyn, etc. |
| **postcode** | ZIP code |
| **deadline** | Lottery end date (application deadline) |
| **unit_type** | Available unit types (Studio/1BR/2BR/3BR) |
| **ami_tier** | ELI, VLI, LI, or combinations |
| **income_range_min** | Minimum income for this tier |
| **income_range_max** | Maximum income for this tier |
| **date_applied** | When you submitted your application (fill in) |

## 11. Tips and Strategy

### 1. Apply to Everything You Qualify For

The more lotteries you enter, the better your chances. In HPD's most recent fiscal year, approximately 9,300 people were selected for new units — out of millions of applicants. Treat applying as an ongoing habit, not a one-time effort.

**Action items:**
- Check Housing Connect weekly for new listings
- Apply to every listing where your income and household size qualify
- Don't limit yourself to one neighborhood or borough
- Apply to waitlists too — they can take years, but they do move

### 2. Lower AMI Tiers Have Better Odds

Extremely Low-Income (30% AMI) and Very Low-Income (50% AMI) units receive fewer applications than Moderate or Middle-Income units. If your income qualifies you for a lower AMI tier, your odds are significantly better.

**Why:** Fewer households have incomes low enough to qualify, so the applicant pool is smaller.

**Action items:**
- If your income drops (job loss, reduced hours), update your Housing Connect profile
- Apply to ELI/VLI units whenever you qualify
- Don't ignore LI (80% AMI) units — they're still more competitive than Mod/Mid

### 3. Keep Documents Updated

When your number is called, you'll need current documents. If your pay stubs are 6 months old, you'll scramble to get new ones — and may miss the deadline.

**Action items:**
- Scan and store new pay stubs every month
- Update tax return copies annually (after you file)
- Keep benefit award letters current
- Maintain a digital folder: "Housing Connect Documents"
- Back up everything to cloud storage

### 4. Check for New Listings Weekly

New lotteries are posted on Housing Connect regularly — sometimes multiple per week. Deadlines can be as short as 30 days.

**Action items:**
- Set a weekly calendar reminder (e.g., every Sunday at 10 AM)
- Bookmark housingconnect.nyc.gov and check for new listings
- Follow @NYCHousing on social media for announcements
- Sign up for HPD's email newsletter at nyc.gov/site/hpd

### 5. Use Community Board Preference to Your Advantage

The community board preference is the single most powerful tool in the lottery system. If you live in the same community district as a building, you compete for 50% of the units among a much smaller applicant pool.

**Action items:**
- Find your community district at nyc.gov/site/planning/community/community-portal.page
- When browsing listings, note the community board number
- Prioritize applications in your own community district
- If you're planning to move within NYC, consider moving to a community district with many upcoming affordable housing projects (e.g., CB4 Hell's Kitchen, CB12 Washington Heights/Inwood, CB1/4 Bushwick/Williamsburg)

### 6. Be Honest and Consistent

Any discrepancy between your application and your documents will cause delays or rejection. Common pitfalls:
- Reporting net income instead of gross (pre-tax) income
- Forgetting to include a benefit or side income
- Not listing all household members
- Using a nickname instead of your legal name

**Action items:**
- Report GROSS (pre-tax) income, always
- Include every source of income, no matter how small
- List every person who will live in the unit
- Use your legal name as it appears on your SSN card and ID
- When in doubt, over-disclose — you can explain, but you can't hide

### 7. Prepare for the Credit Check

Affordable housing landlords will run your credit. While standards are more lenient than market-rate, significant issues can disqualify you.

**Action items:**
- Get your free credit report at annualcreditreport.com
- Dispute any errors
- Pay off small collections if possible
- Use Ready to Rent (Section 9) for free credit counseling
- Don't apply for new credit cards or loans before your eligibility review

### 8. Don't Give Up

The median time from application to approval is 191 days, and many applicants wait years on waitlists. Persistence is the most important factor.

**Action items:**
- Keep applying even after rejections
- Update your profile when your situation changes
- Stay on waitlists — they do move
- Use Housing Ambassadors for support
- Consider all pathways: Housing Connect, NYCHA, SPOA, supportive housing

### 9. Avoid Scams

- **NYC Housing Connect is ALWAYS free.** No one should ever charge you to apply.
- **No one can guarantee you an apartment** through the lottery
- **Never pay a "fee" or "deposit"** to hold a unit before you've been through the full eligibility process
- **Verify all communications** come from @hpd.nyc.gov or the official marketing agent
- **Report scams** to 311 or the NYC Department of Consumer and Worker Protection

### 10. Get Help When You Need It

- **Housing Ambassadors** — Free application assistance (Section 8)
- **Ready to Rent** — Free financial counseling (Section 9)
- **Homebase** — Homelessness prevention and housing navigation (311 or nyc.gov/homebase)
- **HPD hotline** — 212-863-7990
- **Legal Aid Society** — Free legal help for housing issues: 212-577-3300
