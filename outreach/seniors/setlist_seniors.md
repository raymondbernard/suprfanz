# Suggested Setlist for Senior Audiences

## Philosophy
Senior audiences respond best to **familiar music** — songs they grew up with, can sing along to, and that trigger positive memories. Mix blues classics with jazz standards, soul, and popular favorites.

## Set 1: Warm-Up (15 min) — Acoustic Blues Classics
1. **Sweet Home Chicago** — Robert Johnson (blues standard, upbeat)
2. **Got My Mojo Working** — Muddy Waters (crowd pleaser, rhythmic)
3. **Hoochie Coochie Man** — Muddy Waters (classic blues, playful)
4. **Everyday I Have the Blues** — B.B. King (smooth, recognizable)

## Set 2: Golden Oldies (15 min) — Soul & R&B Favorites
5. **Hit the Road Jack** — Ray Charles (sing-along, fun)
6. **I Can't Stop Loving You** — Ray Charles (ballad, emotional)
7. **A Change Is Gonna Come** — Sam Cooke (powerful, hopeful)
8. **Stand By Me** — Ben E. King (universal, sing-along)
9. **My Girl** — The Temptations (upbeat, joyful)

## Set 3: Jazz Standards (15 min) — Smooth & Swing
10. **Route 66** — Nat King Cole (swing, travel theme)
11. **What a Wonderful World** — Louis Armstrong (beloved, emotional)
12. **Fly Me to the Moon** — Frank Sinatra (swing, recognizable)
13. **Blue Moon** — Classic standard (ballad, nostalgic)

## Set 4: Blues Finale (15 min) — Energy + Requests
14. **The Thrill Is Gone** — B.B. King (iconic blues, dramatic)
15. **At Last** — Etta James (romantic, sing-along)
16. **Proud Mary** — Tina Turner/CCR (energetic finale)
17. **Sweet Home Chicago** (reprise if crowd loves it)

## Encore / Requests
- Open the floor for resident requests
- Common requests: Elvis, Johnny Cash, Patsy Cline, Dolly Parton
- Have 10-15 additional songs ready as backup

## Tips
- **Keep volume moderate** — senior ears are sensitive
- **Take breaks between sets** — chat with residents, share stories
- **Encourage sing-alongs** — lyrics are often familiar
- **Watch the audience** — if they're tired, slow down; if energized, pick up tempo
- **Dress sharp** — seniors appreciate presentation
- **Stay after** — residents love to talk and share their own music memories
